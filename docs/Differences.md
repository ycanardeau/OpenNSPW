# Differences from NSPW 1.22
A single-player game plays a scenario of NSPW 1.22 (`Original/Nspw122/NSPW_122.exe`) against its computer. It runs 1.22's own code, in the emulator ([Singleplayer.md](Singleplayer.md)), for the scenarios, the computer (`cpu_strtgy`, `set_cpu_route`, [ComputerPlayer.md](ComputerPlayer.md)) and the victory conditions (`decision`). Everything else is NSPW NET's code: the battle (`chara_cont` and the functions it calls), the input, the drawing and the data; the frame is 1.22's. Where the battle of 1.22 differs from NSPW NET's, a single-player game plays by 1.22's rules, which `BattleRules` (`OpenNSPW/OpenNspw/BattleRules.cs`) gathers, each with the address it was read at, and network games by NSPW NET's.

This document lists every difference found between the two, read from 1.22's machine code, with the address where it shows; [Verification.md](Verification.md) checks the port against 1.22 itself. The single-player game follows 1.22 in the first table only; the others are left as NSPW NET has them, so that the port keeps a single battle, the one network games play.

Supply and ships outside the map together let the computer's fleets renew. In scenario 304 the American naval base (unit 21) is at x 10600, outside the map. A fleet that has lost its carrier, its ammunition or its fuel returns there ([ComputerPlayer.md](ComputerPlayer.md), `cpu_strtgy` step 9); there it supplies, which gives back its HP, ammunition and fuel, and step 12 puts a new carrier at its supplying flagship. NSPW NET's battle would turn the fleet back at the edge, and stop its supply away from a base within 280.

How the off-map rule was found: the port's `em_of_out_of_map` sends a ship to 9560 or 7160 (`MAP_RIGHT-40`, `MAP_TOP-40`). In 1.22 those values are immediate operands (`mov dword ptr [esi+0x491b6c], 0x40c2ac00`, the high half of 9560.0) at `0x416B5C` to `0x416C8B`, after `cmp word ptr [esi+0x4916a0], cpu_side` / `je` at `0x416B3C`, so a search of the data for the doubles does not find them.

## The battle
What 1.22's `chara_cont` (`0x401000`) and the functions it calls do differently from the port's battle (`UpdateBattle` and the functions it calls), read instruction by instruction.

| Difference | 1.22 | NSPW NET |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `set_new_unit_plane` with no parking place left (`0x41F111`) | The place number of the previous plane added, or, for the first, whatever its stack holds | `AddPlane`: 0, or the previous plane's. Single-player never calls `AddPlane`: the scenarios and `cpu_strtgy` call 1.22's own, in the emulator, so only what the emulator's stack holds there can differ from Windows, where it depends on earlier calls |

Compared and the same: reloading and rearming (`0x403043` to `0x4030B6`), the damage that doubles the firing intervals (below 30% of the HP, `0x4030B6`), and the evasions of torpedoes and planes (`set_pos_of_emrgncy_SHIP`, `0x415F4F` to `0x4160FD` and `0x416501` to `0x41688B`).

Compared with `fire_now` (`0x40FE10`), the fires (`0x418C30`) and the emergency moves of every kind, beyond the rows above, and found the same: the shells, the ships' and planes' machine guns, the ships' and planes' torpedoes but the reload, the bombs, the cargo landing, and the emergency moves of planes and transports. Floating-point expressions such as `x*0.1` where the port has `x/10` can differ in their last bit; they are left as NSPW NET has them.

| Difference | 1.22 | NSPW NET |
| --- | --- | --- |

| The unit panel of a plane that crashes, or of a submerged submarine that sinks | Keeps the unit (`0x41B113`, `0x41BC1F`); only a surfaced ship that sinks clears it (`0x41BEDB`) | Cleared (`unit_info[0]=0`). What 1.22's panel then shows (`0x430B40`) is not compared yet |

### 1.22's frame
1.22 is driven by a multimedia timer, `timeSetEvent(88 × wPeriodMin, wPeriodMin, 0x4329B0, 0, TIME_PERIODIC)` (`0x432960`); with the usual minimum period of 1 ms, every 88 ms. The callback counts the timer's ticks (`0x483B50`) and posts `WM_USER` once; the window procedure handles it (`0x433C61`) by drawing a frame (`0x434570`) and clearing the count.

A frame of the battle:

1. If the speed (`0x489FA8`) is not 0, `chara_cont` and `decision` run (speed + ticks counted − 1) times, then `chara_cont` once more with `0x48A1EC` set, which marks the tick that is drawn: on that tick only, the view scrolls (`0x430150`) and returning planes adjust their approach to their moving carrier (`0x417530`). At speed 0 no `chara_cont` runs, but the rest of the frame does.
2. `0x4312A0`, the drawing of the battle area (`draw_cmbt_area`, `0x40DB50`), `0x430B40`.
3. `decision` (`0x41C3C0`).
4. Unless the game has ended, the orders (`0x430390`): `cpu_strtgy` (`0x404E30`) when the computer plays, then the player's.

The speed starts at 1 (`0x42A148`), so two ticks run in a frame of 88 ms. The count of the timer's ticks is 1: it is cleared after the frame, so the ticks while a frame runs are lost, and a frame starts on the next.
